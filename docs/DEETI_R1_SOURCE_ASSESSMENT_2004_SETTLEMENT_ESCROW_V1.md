## Source identity

Babcock, Linda & Landeo, Claudia M. (2004), “Settlement escrows: an experimental study of a bilateral bargaining game,” *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 53(3), 401–417. DOI: 10.1016/S0167-2681(03)00090-8.

## What the study establishes

The published abstract reports a controlled bargaining experiment examining a settlement-escrow institution under uncertainty. It reports significantly higher settlement rates when settlement escrow is added under asymmetric information, and a positive effect on outcome quality measured as the percentage of true damage represented by the outcome. citeturn0search0turn0search3

The paper therefore provides a strong **experimental design precedent** for testing whether an escrow mechanism can change transaction/settlement outcomes.

## DEETI relevance

### Strengths

- experimental manipulation of escrow versus a baseline condition;
- controlled uncertainty conditions;
- observable settlement outcomes;
- measurable timing and outcome-quality variables;
- directly relevant to the proposed generic escrow transaction environment.

### Primary-source blockers

The available source description does not establish that Trust, Transparency, and Verified Fulfilment were separately operationalized as independent constructs. The study was designed to test a specific litigation settlement institution, not the DEETI Escrow Trinity construct. citeturn0search0turn0search4

Therefore this source cannot currently be treated as a PRIMARY_ELIGIBLE DEETI dataset.

## Decision

**SECONDARY_ONLY / DESIGN PRECEDENT**

Use for:

1. historical evidence that escrow can be experimentally manipulated;
2. precedent for treatment/control construction;
3. precedent for settlement-rate and timing outcomes;
4. theoretical background for the new DEETI experiment.

Do **not** use it as confirmatory evidence for:

`ET = Trust + Transparency + Triumph → DE foundational capability`.

## Why this strengthens the research

The study reports positive effects in its own defined institutional context, but that does not establish a universal escrow effect or the DEETI construct. This distinction preserves falsifiability and prevents confirmation bias.

## Next requirement

A new primary experiment must explicitly operationalize:

`Trust + Transparency + Verified Fulfilment`

before O1 is observed, while holding the underlying economic task constant between treatment and control.
